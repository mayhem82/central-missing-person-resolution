Advice levels

What does this mean?

Level 2

Exercise a high degree of caution in China overall

Level 3

Reconsider your need to travel to Gyirong County, in Tibet, China, due to the ongoing risk of severe flooding and landslides.

Level 2

Exercise a high degree of caution in Nepal overall. 

Level 3

Reconsider your need to travel to flood affected areas in the vicinity of the Bhote-Koshi and Trishuli rivers in Rasuwa, Nuwakot, Dhading, Chitawan, Tanahu, Nawalpur and Gorkha Districts, due the risk of landslides and further flooding.

Current situation

Severe flooding and landslides have caused significant damage to roads and infrastructure in the affected areas. Due to the damage and high risk of further severe flooding and landslides, we advise you to reconsider your need to travel to:

  • Gyirong County, in Tibet, China
  • flood-affected areas surrounding the Bhote-Koshi and Trishuli rivers in Rasuwa, Nuwakot, Dhading, Chitwan, Tanahu, Nawalpur and Gorkha Districts in Nepal.

Damage assessments are ongoing. The following essential services are disrupted:

  • transportation
  • power distribution
  • water and food supply
  • phone and internet networks
  • emergency services
  • medical care.

Consular officials are working urgently with authorities, tour operators and international partners to locate and confirm the welfare of Australians who may have been in the affected area.

Australian crisis response staff have been deployed to the region. Australia is coordinating with the Government of Nepal, the United Nations, the Red Cross and other organisations on the humanitarian response.

Full advice

Australians in Nepal and Tibet, China

If you are in an affected area

If you’re in an area that has been directly affected by flooding and landslides:

  • follow the advice of local authorities
  • contact your family, next of kin or travel provider to let them know you’re safe
  • postpone overland travel between Nepal and Tibet, China, including Mount Kailash and Lake Manasarovar circuits.

There is a high risk of further flash flooding and landslides. Stay on high ground, away from:

  • riverbanks
  • low-lying areas and river valleys
  • areas that are showing landslide warning signs. 

Roads, transport services and infrastructure in the affected area have been significantly damaged.

If you're travelling as part of a tour, contact your tour operator or travel agent for updates and support.

Read our advice on:

  • staying safe when there's a natural disaster
  • what to do if there's a landslide or mudslide while you're overseas
  • what to do if there's a flood while you're overseas

Read and subscribe to our travel advice for:

  • Nepal
  • China

If you are outside of the affected areas

If you have recently travelled in Tibet, China or Nepal, please contact your family, next of kin or travel provider to let them know you are safe. They may be concerned about your safety and may not know your current whereabouts.

Reconsider travel to flood-affected areas. Roads, transport and local services may be disrupted, and there’s an ongoing risk of flooding and landslides. Additional visitors may place pressure on emergency workers and local communities.

Due to the recent flooding and landslides, road access to remaining land border crossings between Tibet, China and Nepal may be unsafe. If you choose to travel by vehicle to a land border crossing, ensure your guide or driver exercises a high degree of caution. If you need to leave Tibet, commercial flights continue to operate from Lhasa and Shigatse.

Monitor local media. Follow the instructions of local authorities.

Transport services may be disrupted or cancelled. Check with your airline, tour operator, travel agent or other travel provider before departure to confirm schedules and travel arrangements before going to the airport or station.

If you can’t continue your trip, or your planned route travels through an affected area, contact your travel provider to see if they can help you, or your insurer to discuss the assistance available under your policy. 

If you have travel insurance, contact your insurer even if you are unsure you have a claim. Cover may be available for travel disruption, and your insurer's emergency assistance team may be able to help in other ways, such as arranging evacuation or rebooking flights and accommodation.

We continue to update our travel advisories for Nepal and China with destination-specific information. Subscribe to get email alerts when any advice changes.

If you’re concerned about someone in the affected areas

Reconsider travel to the affected areas to look for someone who is out of contact.

We are making every effort to confirm the welfare of Australians known to be in the affected area. We have assigned a dedicated and experienced case officer to each next of kin of missing Australians. They are providing ongoing support and a consistent point of contact.

Phone and internet services may still be unreliable in the affected areas. 

If you’re concerned about an Australian:

  • keep trying to contact them via phone, text, email and social media
  • reach out to their family, friends, travelling companions or their tour company.

You can also contact the Consular Emergency Centre on:

  • +61 2 6261 3305 (if you're overseas)   
  • 1300 555 135 (in Australia).

Travelling to Nepal or China

If you have an existing booking

The humanitarian impact of this disaster is expected to be significant. Local infrastructure and services will be affected for some time. Be respectful of the ongoing impacts on local communities. 

If you’re planning to travel to Nepal or Tibet, China, in the next week:

  • contact your tour operator or travel agent to ask if your trip is affected
  • consider delaying your trip or changing your plans to avoid the affected areas.

If your trip is in the coming weeks or months, wait for your travel provider to contact you before making changes. 

If you're cancelling or changing your trip, contact your travel insurer to check what your policy covers. Speak with your tour operator or airline about refunds or rebooking options.

Travel insurance bought before the flooding and landslides occurred is more likely to cover related disruptions. Contact your insurer to understand how your policy applies to your circumstances.

If you're considering travel to Nepal or Tibet, China

Reconsider travel to flood-affected areas. Travelling to these areas may place further pressure on local response efforts and limited services.

Access to affected areas is limited. Reliable information may be difficult to get while damage assessments continue.

If you decide to travel to Nepal or Tibet, China, read the latest Smartraveller advice and subscribe for email updates. 

Before you travel:

  • check visa requirements
  • understand safety risks
  • ensure you have appropriate travel insurance
  • save emergency contact numbers in your phone and print a hardcopy to take with you.

Humanitarian aid

Information on Australia’s humanitarian assistance and how you can help is available on the DFAT website.   

Only contribute through reputable, verified organisations like the Nepal-Tibet Flood Disaster - Emergency Action Alliance. Scammers may take advantage of major disasters through fake fundraising appeals.
